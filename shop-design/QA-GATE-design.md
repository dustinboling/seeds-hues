## 4. Findings

### F-1 — MAJOR — Ada's Ruling 7 focus landing exists only in ADA-RULINGS; the SPEC's behavior contract and §13 checks are silent where the control unmounts itself

**Object:** SPEC §5.5, §5.7, §13; UI-DESIGN §7.2; ADA-RULINGS Ruling 7.
**Evidence:** §3 above (full-text verification). SPEC §4 states the general contract — "the activated control keeps focus and stays mounted" — which is precisely what a remover or Clear All activation makes impossible, and §5.7's one exception covers only the case where the row survives. The un covered cases are the common ones: every remover tap and every Clear All on this page unmounts its own control.
**Expected vs actual:** The assembled design, read whole, contains the correct ruling (Ada's: Clear All if the row remains; else the "{name}'s shop" `<h2>` made programmatically focusable with a visible ring under plain `:focus`). The build-facing record — the SPEC a brief lifts from (§14: "build brief lifted from §§3–11") and the SH series that gates the build — contains neither the ruling nor a check that could catch its absence. A faithful build from the SPEC ships focus falling to `<body>` on the page's most repeated recovery action: a keyboard shopper clearing a filter is silently returned to the top of the document. That is a 2.4.3 focus-order failure in substance, designed-in by omission — the same failure shape as Tranche 3's F-1/F-2, where the contract's silence, not its text, was the defect.
**Standard violated:** Internal completeness of a normative contract (the SPEC's own §0.3/§1 standard: behavior contracts stated as observables, checkable in §13); WCAG 2.2 AA 2.4.3 as ruled by the floor's owner.
**Required amendment:** A-1 (§7).
