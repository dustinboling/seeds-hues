# Seller-as-Shop — Accessibility Rulings (Ada)

**Seat:** Ada, Accessibility — owner of the WCAG 2.2 AA floor. **Date:** 2026-11-22.
**Subject:** The assembled seller-as-shop design — Nadia's SPEC (`shop-design/SPEC.md`), Mina's UI design (`shop-design/UI-DESIGN.md`), Wren's copy rulings (`shop-design/COPY.md`), Elena's art consultation (`shop-design/ART-CONSULT.md`, concurrence; nothing in it touches this floor beyond what Ruling 12 records) — against the criteria named in each ruling below.
**Method:** every contrast pair in Mina's §12 was recomputed by this seat from the shipped hex values (`color.paper` `#F8F1E1`, `color.surface` `#FFFDF6`, `color.ink` `#2E2A23`, `color.ink-soft` `#4A4438`, `color.muted` `#6A5F4C`, `color.line-strong` `#91825C`, `color.leaf` `#1E4634`, `color.leaf-600` `#2E6B4F`, `color.leaf-50` `#E3EAD8`, `color.link` `#1F5C3D`, `color.focus` `#7A3E10`, `color.warn` `#8A5E08`, white `#FFFFFF`) using the WCAG relative-luminance formula — linearize sRGB, weight 0.2126 / 0.7152 / 0.0722, (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05). Unrounded values decide; four decimal places are recorded. Floors: 4.5:1 normal text (1.4.3), 3:1 large text and non-text — component boundaries, states, meaningful graphics, focus (1.4.11). Mina's arithmetic was not taken on faith; it was reproduced (Ruling 1). The interaction rulings are made against the behavior contracts as written in the four documents; no part of the assembled design has yet been built, so nothing below claims a rendered, screen-reader, forced-colors, or zoom proof — those are Quinn's gate checks (Nadia SH-12, SH-14, and the checks named in Rulings 10–11), and the conformance sentence at the end of this document says exactly that.
**Conformance language:** these rulings are engineering evidence that the design, as specified, meets the WCAG 2.2 AA criteria named. Built to WCAG 2.2 AA, not formally certified — certification requires a specialist firm testing with assistive technology and disabled users, and the real-AT session list remains open.

## Ruling 2 — The contact block's semantics (SPEC §3.5.2; UI §4.2; COPY §1, CS-01…CS-03): **APPROVED as designed**

The structure Nadia specced and Wren ruled is sound, and each question put to this seat is answered in turn.

**Link purpose in context (2.4.4) — passes, by the composition Wren ruled.** Each row is **one** `<a>` whose text is label + destination: "Customer service {phone}" (`tel:`) and "Customer service {email}" (`mailto:`). Purpose is determinable from the link text alone, without reaching for surrounding context: the label names whose channel it is and what kind of channel the data guarantees it is (SPEC §3.5.5 admits only seller-published customer-service contacts, so the label is a fact about the data, not a guess), and the value is the destination itself, in full. The one-link composition is load-bearing and is a binding build obligation, not a styling choice: were the label left as plain text outside the link, the link text would be a bare number or address — a destination with no stated purpose — and this ruling would not cover it. Two corollaries of the same obligation: no `aria-label` may override the rows' names (an override that dropped the visible value would break both 2.4.4 and 2.5.3, label-in-name — the accessible name must contain the visible label and value, in order, as the one-link text gives it for free), and the value's underline (Mina §4.2) must persist in every state — it is the non-color cue that identifies the value as the link's operative part (1.4.1; the link color against the label beside it measures only 1.2213:1, so color could never carry that identification alone, and under this design it is never asked to: underline + icon + hover change + focus ring carry it redundantly).

**The shared label across two different destinations — ruled sufficient.** Both rows begin "Customer service", and that is not a purpose failure; it is the correct half of the pattern. 2.4.4 asks whether *each* link's purpose is determinable from its text together with its context. The full texts differ exactly where the destinations differ — a phone number and an email address are self-describing in different modalities, unmistakable to sight, to a screen reader's links list, and to voice control — so no two links on the page share a name, and no name misdescribes its destination. The shared prefix is consistent identification (3.2.4) doing its proper work: the same concept, the seller's customer-service channel, named the same way twice. Inventing two label wordings to manufacture a difference the values already state would weaken, not strengthen, the pattern. **Approved: the shared label satisfies purpose-in-context, on condition the values remain in the link text in full** — which is SPEC §3.5.1's visible-destination principle, and which Wren's composition preserves.

**Tap targets (2.5.8; the house 44px standard) — pass with room.** Each row is a full-row link at `min-height: 44px` (UI §4.2, §16 component tokens), far past the 24×24 AA floor and at the 44px size this seat designs toward. The two rows stack with no gap; that is compliant, because a 24px-diameter circle centered in either row cannot reach the other row's center 44px away — the 2.5.8 spacing test is met by the rows' own height. The 32rem width cap at `sm` and up (Mina PB-M4) is accessibility-neutral: the target remains 44px tall and hundreds of pixels wide on every surface, and is full-width on the phone, the primary surface. Long values grow the row (UI §4.3); 44px is a minimum everywhere in the block, never a fixed height — which is also what Ruling 11 relies on.

**No-JS behavior — pass, unconditionally.** The rows are plain server-rendered `<a href="tel:…">` / `<a href="mailto:…">` elements: no component state, no hydration dependency, no enhancement layer. They render and operate identically with JavaScript disabled, before hydration, and in the installed PWA (SPEC §§3.5.2, 10.4). Neither opens a new tab — correct: `tel:`/`mailto:` hand off to the phone/mail application, there is no document to tab, and a `target="_blank"` here would be a defect, not a courtesy. The `href` carries the dialable form (country code + digits) while the visible text is the number as the seller publishes it (SPEC §3.5.2); those two must describe the same number — Quinn's SH-14 already checks exactly that, and this ruling adopts it as the floor's check too.

**Focus** is the global ring (focus on paper, 7.3932:1, C-21) on rows sitting in open flow with page padding around them, so ring + offset are never clipped (UI §4.2) — the same composed treatment approved in Tranche 3 Ruling 6. The PhoneIcon/MailIcon glyphs are `aria-hidden` redundant channels beside label + value; Mina's fallback (a row ships without a glyph that fails the family-hand check, never with a substitute) is accessibility-neutral and is approved — the channel is complete without the glyph, so the fallback can never strand a meaning.

One considered point on CS-01, recorded so it is not mistaken for an oversight: the website button's ruled label, "Visit their website ({host})", drops the seller's name the shipped label carried. Its purpose remains determinable (2.4.4): the button sits inside the `<article aria-labelledby="seller-title">`, so the seller's identity is the link's programmatic context, and the host — the information that distinguishes this destination — is in the name itself, including in a links list. Approved as ruled by Wren.

## Ruling 3 — The did-you-mean inline link (UI §10.3): **APPROVED as designed — explicitly NOT overruled; Mina's justification is replaced, her design stands**

Mina admits one sub-44px target — the did-you-mean candidate link inside its sentence in the searched-empty state — "on the shipped precedent of the zero-shop state's inline 'Browse all varieties' link," and invites this seat to overrule. **The invitation is declined, but the precedent is not the reason, and it is struck from the justification.** A shipped sub-44px link is evidence that a pattern exists, not that it conforms; had precedent been the only ground offered, this ruling would have had to prescribe the 44px fix. The design survives on the criterion's own text:

1. **2.5.8 (Target Size — Minimum, AA) is met twice over.** First, by geometry: the link sits in serif body text at 17px/1.6, so its height is its line box, **27.2px — above the 24px floor on its own** — and its width is the candidate's name, a word of ordinary length, not a glyph. Second, independently, by the criterion's **inline exception**: a target in a sentence, or whose size is otherwise constrained by the line-height of non-target text, is excepted from the 24px requirement. An inline suggestion link inside its own sentence is the exception's named case.
2. **2.5.5 (Target Size — Enhanced, AAA) — the source of the 44px figure — carries the same inline exception.** The house 44px standard in my playbook is "design toward 44 where the layout allows"; a sentence is precisely where the layout does not allow it without breaking the text the link lives in, and enlarging this link into a chip or button would also falsely promote a tertiary suggestion to the rank of the recoveries, damaging the state's hierarchy (UI §10.3's reasoning, which is correct).
3. **The surrounding facts that make the exception honest all hold in this design** and are conditions of this approval: the link is a *suggestion, not a recovery* — both primary recoveries (the search remover and Clear All) are present at full 44px in the applied row directly above it, and the search field itself remains above them, so a visitor who cannot or will not tap the small link loses nothing but a shortcut; the link stands alone on its line (8px below the line above, the claim line a full 32px step below the region), with **no adjacent target** it could be confused with or mistapped into; and its text is the candidate's name, so its purpose is determinable in context (2.4.4) — on the binding condition that **the candidate link and the words "Did you mean" remain in the same paragraph element**, so the context a screen reader presents is programmatic, not merely visual proximity.

**Conditions, stated once:** the approval covers the link *as an inline link in its sentence at the shipped line-height*. If any future change extracts it into a standalone control, shrinks the line-height below 24px, or removes either 44px recovery from above it, the exception and this ruling lapse together and a 44px target becomes mandatory without a new commission. The zero-shop "Browse all varieties" link stands under the same analysis (inline, in its sentence, self-describing) — as a fellow instance of the exception, not as anyone's precedent.

## Ruling 4 — Filter strips: focus, state, and counts (SPEC §§4, 5.2–5.4; UI §6.3): **APPROVED as designed**

- **Focus is never dropped (2.4.3, 2.4.7, 3.2.2).** Under SPEC §4's contract the activated chip stays mounted, keeps focus, and acknowledges in the same frame; results, counts, applied row, and count line update around it. The single stated exception — an action that unmounts its own control in the zero-combination state, landing focus on the applied row's Clear All (SPEC §5.7, applying Tranche 3 §2.6.3) — is approved as the inherited, bounded exception it is; Ruling 7 completes the same discipline for the removers, where the documents left it unstated. No other focus movement occurs on any filter action, and focus never falls to `<body>` from a strip.
- **Selected state never rides on color alone (1.4.1, 1.3.1).** A selected chip carries leaf text (C-17) + leaf border + leaf-50 ground + weight 600 + the programmatic state (`aria-current` before hydration / without JS, `aria-pressed` after — SPEC §5.4) — five channels, of which at least three (border, weight, programmatic state) survive any color-vision deficiency and forced-colors mode (Ruling 10). Hue's swatch dots are `aria-hidden` redundant channels beside the family name that carries the meaning; the "Mixed" conic is a specimen, never a signal.
- **Counts are perceivable in both modalities (1.3.1, 4.1.3's discipline applied to content).** The visible numeral (muted; C-12 at rest, C-19 under hover/selected — both past 4.5:1 at 14px) and the screen-reader unit ("{count} listings", CS-18) are one fact in two renderings. Binding obligation, restated from Tranche 3 §2.4.2 which this design inherits: the count belongs to the chip's accessible description of the option, so a screen-reader shopper comparing options hears the same arithmetic a sighted shopper sees — a count present only as an `aria-hidden` visual numeral would fail this ruling. Zero-count options stay operable (SPEC §5.3): a control that vanished or disabled itself when its count reached zero would break focus predictability and hide the very combination the shopper is constructing; operability is the accessible behavior, approved.
- **Group semantics.** Legends are the model's verbatim eyebrow legends over grouped controls, on the `BrowseFilters`/`FilterStrip` machinery whose grouping (1.3.1) this seat has already passed on `/variety`; the shop adds no new grouping pattern and inherits no new obligation. A group that cannot discriminate in a shop is absent, never disabled (SPEC §5.2) — correct: a disabled control announces a state it cannot explain, an absent one makes no claim.
- **Strip mechanics at the floor.** The strips' internal horizontal scroll is the shipped toolbar pattern (see Ruling 11 for the reflow analysis). Within it, the 3.1-F finish this design inherits — 6px list padding so a focused chip's ring shows whole, `scroll-padding-inline: 3rem`, 44px chevrons — is what keeps 2.4.11 (Focus Not Obscured, Minimum) satisfiable; the chevrons are a fine-pointer convenience and must never be the only way to reach a chip: swipe on touch and sequential keyboard focus (with the strip scrolling the focused chip fully into view, as native focus scrolling does against the scroll-padding) are the guaranteed paths. Quinn's SH-12 drive is where the unobscured ring is proven rendered; this ruling approves the specified geometry that makes it provable.

## Ruling 5 — The persistent live region, `#seller-results-count` (SPEC §§3.3, 5.6, 7.3; UI §7.3): **APPROVED as designed**

This is the correct pattern for 4.1.3 (Status Messages), and it is approved in each particular:

- **Persistent, polite, atomic, never remounted** (SPEC §5.6): the region exists in the document before any change, so its first change is announcable; `aria-live="polite"` is the right urgency for results a shopper requested and is waiting to hear; `aria-atomic="true"` makes the whole line — total, ordering phrase, pagination suffix — the announcement, so no state is ever announced as a bare number stripped of the order it counts. The visible text and the announced text are the same text (UI §7.3's unchanging dress is part of this: a region that also flickered visually on every update would tax the magnifier user at the exact moment the screen-reader user is listening).
- **One announcement per deliberate change, and no announcement of intermediates.** No loading, skeleton, or busy state exists in this design (SPEC §4), so there is nothing intermediate to suppress; the region's text changes in step with the visible results, once. During as-you-type search (SPEC §6.2), the text changes per settled (debounced) update while focus stays in the field: that is approved, not merely tolerated — a screen-reader shopper typing a query is owed the narrowed total as it settles, `polite` queuing means the announcements do not interrupt the typing, and the alternative (silence until blur or submit) would leave the results region changing unannounced under a live query, which is the actual 4.1.3 failure this pattern exists to prevent. History discipline (SPEC §10.2) and announcement discipline are separate contracts and this design keeps them so: typing makes one history entry on the deliberate act, and the region speaks per settled update.
- **Back/Forward restoration** (SPEC §10.2) restores selections, query, counts, results, and applied row together; because the restored count line is the live region's text, the restoration is announced by the same mechanism — a restored view a screen-reader user could not hear change would be a silent state swap, and this design does not produce one.
- **Failure does not touch the region.** On a failed in-place update the count text stands with the prior results (SPEC §9.5), so the region is silent and the failure line's `role="alert"` (Ruling 9) is the single announcement. Two channels, never both, never neither — that is the announcement budget for every transition this page can make, and it balances.

## Ruling 6 — Search field labeling (SPEC §6.1; UI §6.2; COPY CS-06/CS-07): **APPROVED as designed — and Mina's choice is made binding over Nadia's alternative**

SPEC §6.1 offered two labelings: a visible scope label, or a screen-reader label plus a scope-carrying placeholder. Mina chose the visible label ("Search {name}'s shop", CS-06, set as the field's `.eyebrow` label above it). **This seat approves Mina's choice and rules the alternative not approved for this build.** Reasons, in criterion order:

1. **A placeholder is not a label.** It is not reliably announced, it is not programmatically associated, and it disappears at the exact moment it matters — once the shopper types, a placeholder-carried scope vanishes, and the field becomes an anonymous box whose results are silently narrowing a shop the shopper may still believe is the whole directory. SPEC §6.1's own obligation ("the scope is perceivable before typing") is necessary but not sufficient; the scope must remain perceivable *while* typing, and only the visible label does that. (The placeholder's own contrast is not the issue: muted on surface is 6.1516:1, C-12 — it passes as text; it fails as a labeling mechanism.)
2. **Label-in-name (2.5.3).** With the visible label as the field's accessible name, a voice-control user says what they see — "Search GeoSeed's shop" — and the name matches. An SR-only label would put the spoken name and the visible presentation in two different registers on a page where every neighboring group is visibly labeled, and an unlabeled field among labeled strips is precisely what reads as the global search this placement exists to disambiguate (UI §6.2's reasoning, adopted).
3. **Cognitive and magnification load.** The label, the field, and the submit button live together in one group (label 4px above the field, button inside it) — a magnified viewport that contains the field contains its scope. Meaning is not exiled from the control it explains.

**Binding build obligations under this ruling:** the eyebrow text must be the field's programmatic label (a `<label>` associated with the input, or an equivalent `aria-labelledby` reference) — eyebrow *styling* on an unassociated `<span>` would satisfy the eye and fail 1.3.1 / 3.3.2 / 4.1.2, and is the one way this approved design can be built wrong; the placeholder (CS-07) is ruled an example, never a carrier of scope or of matching promises (Wren's ruled text honors SPEC §6.3's honesty obligation — no Latin names, no aliases); the submit button's name "Search" (CS-08) is sufficient *because* the labeled form (`role="search"`, the shop's own URL as action) supplies its scope — the button is not approved as a standalone unlabeled control, it is approved as the submit of a labeled search form. The absence of combobox/listbox semantics (SPEC §6.6, PB-S1) is endorsed from this seat without reservation: there is no suggestion panel, so there are no `aria-expanded` / active-descendant obligations to get wrong, and the results region + live region (Ruling 5) is a strictly simpler announcement contract than a combobox would impose on the same list.

## Ruling 7 — Applied-filter removers and Clear All (SPEC §5.5; UI §7.2; COPY CS-12/CS-13): **APPROVED WITH ADJUSTMENT — one adjustment, stated exactly**

What is approved as designed: each remover is one 44px chip target whose visible label ("{Group}: {Value}"; "Search: “{q}”") and accessible name ("Remove the {Group} filter, {Value}"; "Clear the search for {q}") state exactly what the tap undoes (2.4.4); the names are mutually distinct, so a links/controls list never presents five identical "Remove" actions; the visible words both appear, in order, inside the accessible name, so label-in-name (2.5.3) is satisfied in substance for voice control; the × glyph is `aria-hidden` and never separately focusable; Clear All's two ruled variants (CS-13) claim exactly the scope the action clears — groups and query, never sort — which is the honest name for the action. The search remover's inclusion in the row (SPEC §5.5's shop addition) is approved on the same ground: everything shaping the view except ordering is removable from one row, in one dialect.

**The gap:** a remover's activation unmounts the remover — the activated control destroys itself — and Clear All's activation unmounts the whole row including Clear All. SPEC §5.7 governs focus only for the zero-combination case (where the row survives, and Clear All is there to receive focus). For a remover tap that leaves other items applied, for a last-item remover tap that empties the row, and for Clear All itself, **no document states where focus lands** — and the default answer, focus falling to `<body>`, is a focus-order failure in substance (2.4.3): a keyboard shopper who clears a filter and is silently returned to the top of the document has lost their place in the control block they were operating, on the page's most repeated recovery action. The build must therefore adopt exactly this, which extends §5.7's existing landing rather than inventing a new one:

> **On any activation that unmounts the activated control in or from the applied row: if the applied row remains after the update, focus lands on the row's Clear All (SPEC §5.7's landing, generalized); if the row is removed because nothing remains applied, focus lands on the "{name}'s shop" `<h2>`, which the build makes programmatically focusable (`tabindex="-1"`, absent from the tab order) and which takes the global ring under plain `:focus`, not only `:focus-visible` — the summary-box discipline of Tranche 3 Ruling 7, applied for the same reason: a programmatic focus move must be *seen* to land. The count line's announcement (Ruling 5) is unchanged in both cases and is not a substitute for the focus landing.**

Nothing else in §5.5 or §7.2 changes: labels, names, geometry, order, and the row's wrap behavior stand as designed. Quinn's checks gain two observables under this ruling: activating the last remaining remover, and activating Clear All from a combined search + filters state, each leave focus on the shop heading with a visible ring, with exactly one count announcement and no focus visit to `<body>`.

## Ruling 8 — Sort control behavior while a query is active (SPEC §7; UI §6.4): **APPROVED as designed**

While `q` is active the sort group is absent (relevance order owns the list, SPEC §6.3) and returns with its prior selection intact when the query clears. From this floor the arrangement is sound, for three reasons:

1. **The ordering truth is always announced.** The count line states the order the list is actually in, in every state — including "{N} matches for “{q}”, best match first." while the sort group is absent (SPEC §7.3, CS-16). For a screen-reader shopper, the disappearance of a control group is silent by default; here it is not, because the live region's text changes in the same update that unmounts the group, and the new text names the ordering that replaced the control's. Binding obligation: the count-line change and the group's unmounting are one update (SPEC §5.6's in-step rule) — a build that unmounted the group a beat before the line changed would leave exactly the silent gap this approval relies on not existing.
2. **Focus cannot be on the group when it unmounts.** The group disappears only in consequence of query activation, whose focus positions are the search field (typing/submit) or the applied row (remover / Clear All) — never a sort chip. No focus-loss case arises from the unmounting itself; Ruling 7 covers the applied-row origins. When the group returns, it mounts without taking focus — correct: a control reappearing must never steal focus from the field the shopper is typing in (3.2.2).
3. **State is carried redundantly while present.** Exactly one sort chip is selected at all times including the default (UI §6.4), in the inherited chip dialect (border + ground + weight + `aria-pressed`/`aria-current`), under a "Sort" legend that groups it (1.3.1). This seat does not re-open the inherited chip pattern to demand radio semantics for a single-select group: the pattern is the site's one pattern, learned once (SPEC §0.1), its state is programmatically exposed, and consistency of identification is itself an accessibility property (3.2.4). Recorded as considered, not overlooked.

Mina's PB-M1 layout-shift reservation (≈78px of block height appearing and disappearing) is noted: it is a visual-stability cost, not a criterion failure — no content becomes unreachable, no focus is lost, and the shift is explained at the moment it happens by the count line. It does not change this ruling.

## Ruling 9 — Empty-state and failure announcements (SPEC §§5.6, 5.7, 9; UI §§10.2–10.5): **APPROVED as designed**

- **Filtered/searched-to-empty.** The announcement is the count line's "0 listings" through the persistent live region (Ruling 5); the state's primary line (CS-19 as fixed by Wren — Ruling 12 records this seat's concurrence; CS-20/CS-21 for the searched case) is ordinary text in the results region, in document order immediately below the count line, so it is the next thing in reading order for the screen-reader shopper who just heard "0", and the first thing in the results area for the magnifier user. **No second live region or `role="status"` may be added to the state text**: the count already announced the change, and a self-announcing state line would double-announce every emptying — the verbosity defect Tranche 3 Ruling 7 flagged in another component is designed out here, and this ruling keeps it out. The applied row stays visible above the state (the recovery is co-located with the message — label, explanation, and remedy adjacent, as magnification requires), and focus follows §5.7 as completed by Ruling 7.
- **The all-sold-out notice** (SPEC §9.4; UI §10.4; CS-23) takes no live region of its own — correct: it is a server-rendered property of the shop, present at load, never triggered or duplicated by filtering or searching, so there is no *change* for 4.1.3 to announce. Its dress (ink-soft, C-9, one value step below the count line, no icon, ground, or warn/petal color) keeps a fact reading as a fact; nothing about it claims an alert semantics it does not have.
- **Failure of an in-place update** (SPEC §9.5; UI §10.5; CS-24). One line, `role="alert"`, announced once, with the applied row; prior results, counts, URL, and the activated control's reverted state all stand, and **focus stays on the activated control** — the alert announces, it does not relocate (an alert that also moved focus would punish the retry it invites). Because the count line does not change on failure (Ruling 5), the alert is the transition's only announcement. Mina raises whether the field-error glyph should accompany the line as a redundant channel and leaves it to this seat: **ruled not required, and not adopted.** The line is a full sentence in the palette's attention register (warn on paper, 5.0636:1, C-10) carrying `role="alert"` programmatically — text plus semantics is already two channels, and the glyph in its home pattern marks a *field*; borrowing it here would claim a field association that does not exist. Text alone stands, as designed.

## Ruling 10 — Forced-colors mode (UI §§2.2, 11; SPEC §5.4): **APPROVED as designed, with two binding verification conditions**

The design's forced-colors posture is the right construction: no state in the new elements is carried by a shadow or a background alone (UI §2.2 — no new element casts a shadow at all). Selected chips survive on their real 1px border + weight + programmatic state when grounds are remapped; the search field's boundary is a real 2px border; contact channels are label + visible value in real text; the failure and empty states are text; icons drawn in `currentColor` remap with the text they accompany. That is the design approved. Two conditions bind the build and Quinn's gate, because both are places this construction can be silently lost in implementation:

1. **The focus indicator must be outline-carried, never shadow-carried, in forced colors.** The global ring (3px + 2px offset, C-21…C-23) is approved as specified; in forced-colors mode box-shadows are stripped and outlines are not, so the ring's survival there depends on its implementation being an outline (or an equivalent border/outline mechanism). Quinn's forced-colors drive must show the ring on a chip, on the search field, and on a contact row; a ring that vanishes in that mode fails this ruling regardless of its light-mode ratios.
2. **`forced-color-adjust: none` is confined to true color specimens.** The Hue swatch dots (and, inside the untouched cards, artwork and swatches) are specimens — the actual color is the content — and may keep the exemption. It must not be applied to any chip as a whole, the search field, the contact rows, the applied row, or any state-bearing element: exempting an interface element from forced colors re-imposes the author's palette on exactly the users who replaced it because they could not use it. No element in the design as written proposes such an exemption; the condition records that none may be added.

The FilterStrip edge fades deserve one sentence: in forced colors the paper-colored fade gradient is stripped with other backgrounds, and the chips beneath it become fully visible — a loss of decoration, not of information or of any state. Accepted.

## Ruling 11 — Reflow, resize, and text spacing in the control stack (UI §11; SPEC §§5.4, 10.4): **APPROVED as designed — proofs assigned to Quinn's gate, not claimed here**

- **320px reflow (1.4.10).** At 320 CSS px the page's content flows in one direction: header stacks, definition list single-column, contact rows full-width, applied row wraps, cards single-column, headings wrap with `balance`. The one horizontal movement is *inside* the filter/sort strips — a toolbar-pattern component whose chips scroll within their own bounded row, the arrangement WCAG's reflow understanding accepts for components that require their own two-dimensional layout, and the same pattern this seat has passed on `/variety`. No page-level horizontal scrolling, no clipped content, and no essential element pushed off the magnified viewport is introduced by the stack: the search field's submit lives *inside* the field's width (96px right padding is internal, UI §6.2), and the control stack is a gap-based column that closes over absent parts, so no band or state leaves a horizontal remnant.
- **200% resize (1.4.4) and text-spacing overrides (1.4.12).** Nothing in the header, contact block, control block, or states has a fixed height: rows state minimum heights and grow (long email wrap, UI §4.3), chips are content-width inside their scrolling strips (spacing overrides widen chips; the strip absorbs the width — the strip is where 1.4.12 pressure goes to be absorbed rather than to clip), the applied and badge rows wrap, headings wrap. The search field's fixed 48px height is not a violation in waiting: it is a single-line input whose text at 16px with a 1.5 line-height override occupies 24px, half the field — the override budget fits inside the shipped geometry. The one known clipping risk in the system — a focused chip's ring inside a strip — is the shipped 3.1-F fix (list padding + scroll-padding), inherited here.
- **Focus not obscured (2.4.11)** at mobile widths and 400% zoom: no sticky element is introduced by this design, the contact rows and field sit in open flow, and the strips' scroll-padding is the specified mechanism inside the strips. 

This ruling approves the *specified geometry*. It does not report a rendered 320px / 200% / spacing-override test, because no build exists to test; those proofs are Quinn's SH-12 (which already specifies 320px and 200% text, unclipped rings, and the screen-reader announcement count) and are conditions of the gate, not of this document. A build that clips, overlaps, or two-dimensionally scrolls at those settings fails 1.4.10/1.4.12 against this ruling no matter how faithfully it matches the documents' other values.

## Ruling 12 — Everything else in the four documents that touches this floor: **ruled here; nothing further beyond this list**

Considered, and disposed of, so the record is complete rather than silent:

- **Motion (2.2.2, 2.3.1, reduced motion).** The results cascade (220ms, opacity + 6px translate, staggered to a 340ms worst case, UI §9.2) is visitor-initiated feedback, not auto-playing content; it flashes nothing (opacity/transform only, no luminance cycling anywhere near the 2.3.1 thresholds); and the shipped `prefers-reduced-motion` rule removes it entirely rather than softening it. Approved. Chip/button color transitions are transitions, not motion that engages the vestibular system.
- **Heading and landmark structure (1.3.1, 2.4.6).** H1 (seller) → H2 ("{name}'s shop", the section's `aria-labelledby` target) → card headings is unchanged in order and gains no skips; the search eyebrow must not be implemented as a heading (Ruling 6's programmatic-label obligation covers the correct element). Breadcrumbs, the `<article aria-labelledby="seller-title">`, and the definition list's semantics are untouched by the register (SPEC §12) and approved by that invariance.
- **Wren's CS-19 fix** ("No listings in {name}'s shop match those filters.") — **concurred from this seat**: the two sibling empty states now name the shop one way, and consistent identification (3.2.4) is an accessibility property, not only a copy preference. CS-21's typesetting note (standalone line, capital W, no leading ellipsis) has no floor impact either way and stands as Wren ruled it.
- **The no-JS floor's landing position — considered, and no adjustment made.** Floor activations are true navigations (links, a GET form — SPEC §10.4) and land, as navigations do, at the top of the re-rendered page; the catalog section sits below the header, About, and any reviews panel. This seat's Tranche 3 Ruling 9 imposed a fragment landing for failed floor *form submissions*, where an unannounced error was the defect (3.3.1). No error and no unannounced state exists here: the navigation is the shopper's own deliberate act, the landed page's heading structure (H1, then the shop H2) provides the re-location path, and pagination on this page has always behaved identically. The distinction is principled, not convenient — but it is recorded so Quinn and the lead know it was weighed: if the floor drives at the gate show no-JS shoppers losing the section, the Ruling 9 fragment remedy is the standing fix and may be applied under this ruling without returning to this seat.
- **Cards, badges, plates, and the reviews panel** are byte-identical or position-identical under SPEC §12 and are not re-ruled: stock on the cards is dot + word + date (never color alone), the badge's pairs are inside Ruling 1 where the shop recomposes them (C-17), the StatePlate and the A4 StateShape are shipped and untouched (Elena's concurrence and SPEC §9A agree; nothing there is this floor's to move), and "Reviews from Google" keeps its separation and exact label.
- **Pagination and the narrowed-404** (SPEC §10.3) are inherited navigation behavior, unchanged in kind from today's page; no new obligation arises from the shop framing.
- **No dark-mode pairs, no text over imagery, and no new artwork** exist in this design (UI §§2, 15; Elena Ruling 1) — those standing open checks are neither engaged nor closed by this pass, and are stated here so their absence from the rulings reads as "not present in the design," not "forgotten."

**Beyond the items above and Rulings 1–11: nothing further.** No other element, state, or sentence in the four documents touches this seat's floor.
