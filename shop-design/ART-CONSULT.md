# Seller-as-Shop — Art Consultation

**Seat:** Elena, Art Direction / Illustration. **Type:** Concurrence-or-contest pass on Nadia's SPEC (`shop-design/SPEC.md`) and Mina's UI design (`shop-design/UI-DESIGN.md`), per the reconciliation rule (behavior → Nadia, visual system → Mina, artwork → Elena).
**Date:** 2026-11-22 · **Status:** Rulings only. No artwork commissioned, painted, moved, or redesigned by this document.
**Governing references applied:** my playbook (profession first, house standards on top); `THEME-ART-DIRECTION.md` (the Illustrated Seed Catalogue — §3 motif rationing, §4 illustration system, composition standard: flower detail first, plates shown whole); the state-icons placement spec (StatePlate beside the seller header; StateShape placement A4 in the Location line — both shipped).

## Ruling 1 — The no-art position: CONCUR

The shop page wants no artwork it does not already carry. Its art load is already assigned, and each piece already has its job: the StatePlate beside the header does *identify + place* (the header's one picture, per Mina §3.1); the Quiet Ledger cards' rails carry the variety plates — flower-first, shown whole, byte-identical under Nadia's §12 register; the StateShape at A4 carries the state link. The page's job is *find this seller's seed and leave by the right door*, and its composition already delivers that: one focal point (the seller's name), one accent (the leaf button), one picture.

A banner, hero, or spot painting would not fill a gap; it would create a competing focal point above a catalog the visitor came to work in, on a phone-first surface where every screen of painting pushes the first card down. The theme direction is explicit that the UI is the paper the art is printed on and never competes with it — this page's art is the plates, and they are already on it.

Two considered-and-rejected additions, recorded so the absences are decisions:

- **A generic spot painting for sellers with no StatePlate** (no plate for their state, or non-US). Rejected. The placement spec's rule — absence renders nothing, no placeholder or substitute — is artistically correct: a stock garden scene would falsely read as that seller's own identity, and would break similarity across sellers (some headed by their state's plate, others by generic dressing). Mina's §0.3 holds: absence here is composed, not a hole.
- **Spot art for the empty states** (Nadia §9). Rejected for the shop. The theme's empty-state paintings (`empty-search`, `empty-no-sellers`) belong to their named directory and variety placements. The shop's filtered-to-empty and searched-to-empty states are transient, in-place states inside the results region; their job is recovery (the applied row, Clear All), and a painting there would compete with the recovery action and load weight into a state the visitor should pass through in seconds. The 0-listing shop's plain line stands as specced — the shop framing invents no stock and no decoration to apologize for having none.

## Ruling 2 — PB-M5, stem motif on the "{name}'s shop" heading: CONCUR with Mina's rejection

The stem divider is a rationed motif, and rationing is what makes it a motif instead of wallpaper. The theme direction (§3) fixes its placements: under the home hero, above the footer, between prose sections — structural breaths in prose. The shop heading is not a prose break; it is the head of a data component (the catalog and its tools). Dressing it with the stem would spend the motif outside its grammar and dilute it at the placements where it does mean something.

Mina's structural reason is also the art reason, stated in Gestalt terms: a divider groups by separating. A stem above the catalog alone would visually partition the page into *pre-shop matter* (About, the reviews panel) and *the shop* — contradicting the SPEC's §0 stance that the whole page is the shop that happens to open with who runs it. The heading's emphasis by gap, size, and the tool block below is sufficient and honest. The plainness is a position; I hold it with her.

## Ruling 3 — PhoneIcon / MailIcon contact glyphs: CONCUR — interface icons, not an art commission

These are not artwork and I do not claim them. They are 20px legibility marks whose entire content is convention: a handset path and an envelope, drawn to the shipped icon family's grid, live area, stroke, and `currentColor` rules (Mina §4.4). There is no composition to direct, no palette to style, no subject to render faithfully — the craft question is whether they match the family hand, which is a property of Mina's visual system and Quinn's family-hand check, not of illustration. Claiming them as art would add a gate and gain nothing.

One endorsement from this seat: Mina's fallback is correct. If a glyph fails the family-hand check, the row ships **without** it — never with a borrowed glyph, a filled variant, or a small painting in its place. Any of those would break the family's similarity, which is the thing that makes the set read as one set.

## Ruling 4 — Anything else: nothing to change

Plainly: nothing else in either document touches art, plates, or the visual world in a way I would change.

- The StatePlate (placement, 280/300/320px steps, `.art-frame`, whole-image rule) and the StateShape at A4 are protected by both documents and touched by neither. The one-state-outline-per-page rule (Nadia §9A) is also right artistically — a second outline at the shop heading would cheapen A4 into repetition.
- The cards' artwork rail, including its shipped plate-then-motif fallback order, sits inside the byte-identical card contract and is not reopened here.
- No typography is baked into any artwork (there is no new artwork); no image is hotlinked; no plate is cropped, stretched, or re-framed anywhere in either document.
- No conflict between professional practice and house standards arose in this pass: the one place they might have pulled apart — a painterly catalogue's instinct to illustrate a new surface against the house discipline of rationed motifs and whole plates — resolves in the house discipline's favor, and in this case the profession agrees with it: decoration that competes with the subject is cut.

**If Dustin later wants a visual storefront treatment beyond this composition** — that is a new commission with its own brief, thumbnails, and gate (Nadia PB-S2), starting from this seat. It is not a gap in Nadia's or Mina's work, and nothing in this ruling pre-designs it.
