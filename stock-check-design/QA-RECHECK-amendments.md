## 2. Item 2 — Nadia's two flagged adaptations

**(a) Ada §7.5 in stronger form at §4.5 — CONFIRMED, not a send-back.** Ada §7.5 (read at source) was written for the reconciled model, where the notice is standing listing state rendered to every visitor: her rule prohibits inserting it into a non-participant's DOM after their live region registers, because the insertion would announce another visitor's failed check to a bystander. Under Nadia's model the notice is attempt-scoped and never part of a fresh render (D-CSN-4), so non-rendering for non-participants is the model's own consequence, and §4.5's added clause — the build must not insert the notice into a non-participant's DOM after their live region registers — closes the one remaining leak path (a client-side update pushing the notice to a bystander) using Ada's own named mechanism. The strengthening preserves §7.5's intent exactly and conflicts with nothing in §2.4 (participants still receive the notice once, through the announcement path, with the S4 text).

**(b) §6.2 scoping of the re-order ruling — CONFIRMED faithful.** The ruling was made on OQ-CSN-1, whose question was the grouped sort's section boundary and nothing else. Nadia's scoping applies re-order exactly there; under "Name A–Z" a stock flip changes no position in any case, so "unaffected" is a fact, not a choice; under "Recently checked" and on the ledger stack, re-order was never put to Dustin — and a completed check rocketing its card to the top of "Recently checked" is the variant Nadia's original §6.2 analysis named the most disruptive of all. Holding there is recorded as standing "until ruled otherwise," not smuggled in as ruled. The scoping neither extends nor narrows the ruling's own words.

## 3. Item 3 — Priya's three caveats, verified against ENGINEERING's text

**(1) Success timestamp missing from the §4.1 schema — caveat CORRECT.** The `listing_stock_checks` sketch carries `attempted_at` (last attempt of *any* outcome), `outcome` (the *last* attempt's outcome only), `signal`, `http_status`, `state`, `lease_expires_at`, `updated_at`. Once a later attempt fails, the row no longer expresses when the last *successful* check happened — the outcome field is overwritten — yet SPEC §3.1's conjunction has a success term the endpoint must test on every POST and in the §4.2 lease claim. The build needs one additive success timestamp on `listing_stock_checks`, stamped only on `outcome='ok'`. (Dustin's standing additive-plumbing authorization covers the column; no ruling is needed for the field itself.)

**(2) Blocked-click and sweeper stamps must never write the success timestamp — caveat CORRECT.** §5.2 as written: "Blocked clicks *do* stamp `attempted_at`." §4.2's sweeper passage: expired leases are logged `error`/`lease_expired`, "also stamping `attempted_at`." Both stamps target `attempted_at`, so the documents as written do not commit the error — the risk is implementational: if the build keys the new window on a success field but lets either of these paths write it, a blocked click or a dead executor starts a 24-hour clock with no success behind it, and gate defect Q-D7 reappears in the new field. The stamp discipline must be stated in the brief, not left to inference.

**(3) §§6.1/6.3 ceilings false as statements; §6.4's 3,000/day can bind — caveat CORRECT.** §6.1's "Absolute ceiling (ruled by the 24h rule itself): at most one fetch per listing per 24h → ≤1,480 product fetches/day … ≤ ~2,960 outbound fetches/day" derives entirely from the attempt-keyed window. Under Ruling 1 a failed attempt is immediately retryable and every retry is a fetch, bounded only by in-flight deduplication, per-domain pacing, the §6.2 IP buckets, and the §6.4 budget — the §6.1 ceiling is not a ceiling. §6.3's "Repetition is free of effect" and "one enum per listing per day" are likewise false for failed checks. §6.4's 3,000/day was calibrated as "just above the §6.1 absolute ceiling, so it never binds legitimate use" — that premise is gone, and on a pathological day (widespread seller failures, legitimate retries) the budget can bind and degrade the endpoint to `disabled` / last-known state until UTC midnight. The failure mode is the designed graceful degradation and fails closed — sellers and spend stay protected; the cost is a day of reduced freshness, not wrong data.

**Ruling on the set:** the ruling note alone does **not** carry these caveats — it never mentions a success field, a stamp discipline, or §6. The set {ruling note + brief obligations} fully carries them **if and only if** the brief carries conditions C1 and C2 in §4 verbatim in substance. On that basis the caveats are carried, not waived.

**Coordinator's disposition on (3) — judged SAFE, not re-decided.** Not quoting §6.1's ceiling in the brief removes a false bound from the build's premises; keeping 3,000/day for v1 keeps a hard spend/abuse cap whose only failure mode is the designed degradation; re-calibration flagged to Dustin as a separate named item puts a spend-posture number with its owner. The disposition introduces no correctness risk to the build.
