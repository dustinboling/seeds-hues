# Crop Coverage Audit — Seed Directory (seeds.hues.flowers)

**Date:** 2026-11-17 · **Trigger:** Dustin's ruling — *"All cut flower crops need to be in this app"* — after echinacea was found to have no crop in the catalog (its seed-saving guide and pod painting exist in project files with nowhere to live).
**Method:** current catalog read from the live sitemap / crop pages (2026-11-17). Reference universe built from Johnny's Selected Seeds cut-flower category, the ASCFG cut-flower literature (WSU/ASCFG production guide EB1982; ASCFG National Seed Trials coverage), the Ball/ASCFG "Choose the Best Cuts" grower deck, professional seed/plug cut-flower lists (Germania/Gro 'n Sell cut list, Raker-Roberta's 2026 Specialty Cut Flowers program, a 2026 cutting-flowers seed list), and The Gardener's Workshop's cut-flower seed framing. Large-company catalogs (GeoSeed/Ball/Germania) were used as **reference only**, per project rules — they may enumerate crops/varieties, never appear as sellers.
**Scope filter applied:** crops propagated from **seed** and grown for cutting, fresh or dried. Out: bulb/corm/tuber-only crops, division/bare-root-only crops, woody cuts. Borderline crops commonly seed-grown for cutting are **in**, and marked **Borderline**.

## (c) Borderline calls — reasoning

**In, per the brief's borderline rule** (commonly seed-grown for cutting even if also available another way):

- **Ranunculus (#12)** — the commercial standard is corms, and corm-only crops are out of scope. But a genuine seed-propagated cut strain exists: Sakata's **Maché** series was bred from seed specifically for cut-flower production, and seed houses sell Maché/Magic seed explicitly for cuts. In, flagged, with variety enumeration limited to the seed strains — corm varieties must not be enumerated into this crop.
- **Perennial Baby's Breath (#24)** — the catalog's "Annual Baby's Breath" is *Gypsophila elegans*. The florist trade's baby's breath is the perennial *G. paniculata*. Some named forms are vegetative ('Bristol Fairy'), but seed-propagated paniculata lines exist and are sold by professional seed houses. Because the catalog already splits baby's breath by species, the perennial is a distinct missing crop, not a duplicate.
- **Goldenrod (#17)** — named florist material is often rhizome-divided, but the species (*S. canadensis*, *S. speciosa*) are seed-grown, seed is widely sold, and working flower farms cut it fresh and dried. In at species level.
- **Astrantia (#30) and Veronica (#29)** — both sit on the ASCFG popular-cuts list and both have real seed lines in perennial seed catalogs; both are also commonly divided/plugged. In, flagged, because seed propagation is genuine and catalogued.
- **Coleus (#20)** — a foliage cut, not a flower; included because the directory already carries foliage crops (dusty miller, silver lace, red leaf hibiscus, orach) and seed is coleus's normal commercial propagation route.
- **Hollyhock (#31)** — the weakest of the "in" calls: primarily a garden biennial, but a professional cut-flower plug program (Raker 2026) lists it as a cut item, and it is seed-grown. In, flagged, lowest priority of the set.
- **Chinese Lantern (#19), Paper Daisy (#23), Honesty (#18)** — dried-flower crops. The directory already carries dried/everlasting crops (statice, strawflower, xeranthemum, winged everlasting, flax pods), so dried-only seed crops are in scope; all three are seed-propagated standards.

**Considered and held out** (cut flowers in the reference sources, but they fail the seed-grown scope rule):

- **Bulb/corm/tuber-only:** tulip, narcissus/daffodil, hyacinth, lily (note: seed-grown Formosa lily is already in the catalog), gladiolus, freesia, crocosmia, iris, allium, tuberose, calla lily, liatris, and anemone-from-corm (per the brief; seed-grown anemone strains exist but the crop's commercial reality is corms).
- **Division / bare-root / cutting-propagated for cut production:** peony, astilbe, hosta, sedum, lysimachia, Japanese anemone, hellebore, perennial (Montecassino) aster, chrysanthemum, gerbera.
- **Woody cuts** (several appear on the ASCFG popular-cuts list but are shrubs/trees, not seed-grown crops): rose, lilac, hydrangea, spirea, viburnum, prunus, willow.
- **Gaillardia and Tithonia** — seed-grown and occasionally cut, but in the sources checked they present as garden crops without a real cut-flower listing behind them; held unless a seller or cut list surfaces. Revisit if a small farm lists them for cutting.

## (e) Non-cut-flower entries inside the current 93 — for the record only

Per Dustin's earlier ruling, everything in Floret's seed collection stays; **no removals are proposed**. Noted only so the record is honest about what the 93 contains:

- **Tomato** — a vegetable crop, not a cut flower in any source checked.
- **Pea** — vegetable peas (the flowering cut crop, Sweet Pea, exists separately).
- **Ornamental Squash** — decorative gourds; arrangement-adjacent when dried, but entered as a vegetable/decorative crop, not a cut flower.
- **Quinoa** — a grain; its seed heads do see dried-arrangement use, so it sits closer to the line than the three above.
- **Herbs — Basil, Dill, Oregano, Shiso, Cress** — herb crops, but each has genuine, documented use as bouquet foliage/filler on flower farms, so they read as legitimate directory entries rather than strays.
