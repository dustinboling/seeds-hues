## F1 — Counts without the sr-only unit — Rating: 2 (minor)

- **Frequency — high for the affected user class, narrow class.** The defect is not on one chip: `ChipCount` is shared by every counted chip on /variety across all five groups (26 options per the gate record's counts matrix), in both the SSR link form and the hydrated button form, on first paint and after every in-place update. A screen-reader shopper meets it on every filtering decision they preview — and previewing counts before tapping is the stated purpose of the §2.4 feature. For shoppers who do not use a screen reader, the audible defect does not occur at all (the parenthesized numeral is a separate, secondary visual departure from the pattern's bare numeral; it misleads no one about what the number is, because a sighted shopper sees the option, its group legend, and the surface it sits on).
- **Impact — low.** The number itself is announced; what is missing is its unit. In context that unit is strongly recoverable: the chip sits inside a labeled group on a page whose entire result set is varieties, and the surface's persistent count line announces variety totals. A screen-reader shopper hearing "Pink (12)" must supply "varieties" by inference — a real comprehension tax on the exact information this feature exists to deliver, and a deviation from Ada's binding pattern — but it does not block the task, does not misstate the number, and is unlikely to cause a wrong selection, because no competing unit (sellers, crops) is counted anywhere in these chips.
- **Persistence — recurring but learnable.** It recurs on every chip and every visit, which keeps it above cosmetic; but once a shopper has inferred the unit once, the inference carries to every other chip on the surface, so the per-encounter cost drops to near zero after first learning. Persistence of exposure is high; persistence of harm is low.
- **Market impact — small but real.** This is a public surface under a WCAG 2.2 AA floor, and the finding is a departure from the accessibility specialist's prescribed, already-shipped pattern; left standing, it normalizes a divergent count rendering in the shared component every later surface inherits. That is a trust-and-consistency cost, not a user-blocking one.
- **Why not 3:** a major rating needs users delayed or struggling to complete the task. Here the task completes, the data is present and correct, and the missing word is inferable from three independent context cues on the same surface. **Why not 1:** it is not merely cosmetic for the affected class — it degrades the primary information of a headline feature on every encounter, for exactly the users the sr-only unit exists to serve.
- **Confidence: moderate-high.** Existence and scope are measured in code. The impact reading rests on the inferred announcement behavior and on context recoverability, neither of which a live screen-reader session was available to confirm.

## F2 — Applied-row naming deviations — Rating: 2 (minor)

Three deviations in one control cluster, rated as the single consolidated finding Quinn presented: (a) visible remover label is the value only ("Pink") instead of `"{Group}: {Value}"` ("Hue: Pink"); (b) accessible name is "Remove Pink" instead of the group-carrying "Remove the Hue filter, Pink"; (c) the clear control reads "Clear all" instead of "Clear all filters". PR #41's DOM tests pin all three deviating forms.

- **Frequency — moderate.** The applied row appears only when at least one filter is active, and the naming matters at the moment a shopper reviews or removes a selection — a common step in the filtering flow, but one step among several, not every chip encounter. The visible-label deviation (a) meets every shopper who reads the row; the accessible-name deviation (b) meets screen-reader shoppers only; (c) meets everyone and costs almost nothing (below).
- **Impact — low today, by the evidence.** No current value vocabulary overlaps across groups (the gate record states this, and the label sets in `removersFor` confirm disjoint vocabularies — "Pink" can only be a hue), and the row renders inside a group labeled "Applied filters" directly under the strips it summarizes. So today, no shopper — sighted or screen-reader — is actually given a wrong or ambiguous target: "Remove Pink" removes the one thing "Pink" can mean. Removing works; Clear All's scope is unambiguous in a row that contains nothing but filter removers. The harm is prospective and structural: the group name is precisely the disambiguator a screen-reader user needs the first time a value *can* belong to two groups, and this spec's own scope note says the tag filter will inherit this machinery — the ambiguity the clause guards against is scheduled, not hypothetical. That future weight is recorded here as an inferred risk, and it is deliberately **not** counted as current impact; severity rates the problem as it stands.
- **Persistence — recurring, consistent, and encoded.** Every applied-row encounter repeats the deviation, and — the aggravating factor that lifts this cluster above cosmetic — the build's own tests pin the deviating names, so the deviation is not an oversight awaiting a glance but the tested, defended behavior of the component. It will reproduce faithfully on every surface that inherits the pattern until both code and tests change.
- **Market impact — minimal.** No trust, comprehension-of-offer, or public-claim consequence; the cost is pattern inconsistency across surfaces (the "one pattern, learned once" rule in SPEC §0) and the scheduled ambiguity above.
- **Why not 3:** measured against users today, nothing blocks, misleads, or materially delays anyone — the gate record's own impact note concedes no functional ambiguity today, and this seat's code inspection agrees. A major rating would have to rest on the spec's normativity itself rather than on user impact, and this seat's scale rates user impact. **Why not 1:** the cluster is three deviations, not one wording slip; one of them (b) removes information a screen-reader user cannot recover from the control itself; and the test-pinning makes the deviation systemic rather than incidental. Taken singly, (c) alone would be a 1; the cluster as consolidated rates 2.
- **Confidence: moderate.** Existence of all three deviations is measured in code; the disjoint-vocabulary reading is measured from the current label sets. The rating turns on a judgment about how much prospective ambiguity counts — named here as judgment, not measurement — and a rater who weights the inherited-machinery risk as current impact would land one point higher; this seat records that as the live uncertainty in this rating rather than hiding it.
